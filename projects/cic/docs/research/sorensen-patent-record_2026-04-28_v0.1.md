## Analytical Notes

### Career Arc via Patents
Sorensen's patent record maps directly onto Ford's operational history:

**Foundry & Production Era (1929–1935)**
- Conveyer (1929) — assembly line infrastructure
- Commutator segments (1931) — electric motor manufacturing for factory tooling
- Overrunning clutch (1931) — drivetrain components
- Boring machine (1932) — precision machining
- Crankshaft molds (1934) — direct V8 production era; the monobloc casting problem
- IC engine oiling system (1933)

**V8 Production Refinement Era (1935–1939)**
- Piston (1935) — internal engine components
- Welding machine (date TBD)
- Steering wheels (1939) — body/chassis manufacturing

**WWII Industrial Mobilization Era (1941–1946)**
- All seven WWII-era patents cluster 1941–1943 filings
- Supercharger + Turbosupercharger (both filed 1942-02-09 same day) — B-24 Liberator engine program at Willow Run
- Armor plate quenching (1943) — direct tank/armor manufacturing for Rouge plant
- Vehicle frame — Canada (1944) — Canadian war production
- Rotor casting (1941) — electrical machinery for war production
- Bearing (1942) — co-invented with Henry Ford himself

### Key Observations for Documentary

1. **The 1942 cluster**: Seven patents filed in a single year (1942) — the peak of Willow Run's ramp-up. Sorensen was inventing process solutions under maximum production pressure simultaneously.

2. **Supercharger + Turbosupercharger filed same day (1942-02-09)**: These two patents filed simultaneously suggest a coordinated engineering push on B-24 engine performance — likely tied to Army Air Forces requirements for higher-altitude bomber operation.

3. **Henry Ford co-inventor on Bearing (US2334916A)**: The patent record explicitly names H. Ford as a co-inventor. This is rare documentary evidence of direct hands-on collaboration between the two men at the engineering level as late as 1942 — after decades together.

4. **US1821119A — Conveyer (1929)**: This is the earliest confirmed patent. A conveyer system filed in 1929 connects directly to Sorensen's assembly line legacy — the moving line he helped perfect at Highland Park carried forward into the Rouge.

5. **International filings**: US1898696A (commutator segments) was filed simultaneously in Belgium, Germany, France, Great Britain, and the Netherlands — indicating Ford Motor's global manufacturing ambitions in 1931.
