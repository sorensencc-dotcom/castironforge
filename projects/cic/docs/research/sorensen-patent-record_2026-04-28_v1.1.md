## Analytical Notes

### Henry Ford as Co-Inventor — 4 US Patents

| US2334916A | Bearing | 1942-05-04 | — |

Span: 1935–1942. The earliest (Piston mold, 1935) pre-dates Henry Ford's documented cognitive decline — evidence of genuine technical collaboration over a seven-year period. The two 1942 patents (IC engine, Bearing) are from the same period as Willow Run production ramp-up. Henry Ford's last patent co-invention: May 4, 1942 (Bearing). No further co-inventions after that date.

### The May 17, 1943 Filing Cluster — Corrected (Four Patents, Not Three)

Full-text retrieval of US2428674A confirms a fourth Ford Motor Co patent filed May 17, 1943 — by Miller and Scarlett, not Sorensen:

| Patent | Inventors | Domain | Notes |
|--------|-----------|--------|-------|
| US2378044A | Sorensen, McCarroll, Rothenthaler | Armor | Rouge plant; spray-quench hardening |
| US2378045A | Sorensen, Van Ranst | Aviation engine | B-24 engine cylinders |
| US2472317A | Sorensen, Miller | Airframe — fuselage | Fuselage subcomponent strap method |
| US2428674A | **Miller, Scarlett** | Airframe — wing | Wing internal skeleton jig (NOT a Sorensen patent) |

US2428674A is the companion to US2378043A: Sorensen's Oct 1942 patent covers wing skin assembly; Miller and Scarlett's May 1943 patent covers wing internal skeleton construction. Together they form the complete B-24 wing section build sequence, inside out.

### Key Co-Inventor Profiles

**Roscoe M. Smith** — Ann Arbor, MI. Co-inventor: US2378043A, US2383225A (Oct 24, 1942). Role: Willow Run airframe engineering lead alongside Sorensen. Not found in published Willow Run histories. Research priority.

**Logan C. Miller** — Co-inventor: US2472317A (with Sorensen), US2428674A (with Scarlett). Airframe engineering bridge between wing internal structure and fuselage assembly work. Not found in published histories. Research priority.

**George E. Scarlett** — Co-inventor: US2428674A (with Miller). Role: wing internal skeleton fixture design. No other documentation found. Research priority.

**Cornelius Van Ranst** — Co-inventor: US2378045A (cylinder construction, 1943). Background: engineer on Miller racing cars, Cord front-drive. His appearance at Willow Run as engine cylinder co-inventor places elite pre-war engine design talent inside the Ford aviation program.

**Russell H. McCarroll** — Co-inventor: US2400495A (centrifugal casting, 1941), US2378042A (multiple centrifugal casting, 1942), US2378044A (armor plate quenching, 1943). Rouge plant / foundry operations across the war period.

### Serial No. 463,268 — "Mating Fixture" (Abandoned Application)

Referenced in US2428674A ("copending") and US2472317A ("abandoned"). Described how fuselage strap rings are used to mate completed components to adjacent components. Not granted. The missing issued patent for the B-24 fuselage mating step. Worth searching USPTO abandoned application records.

### Career Arc via Patents

**Assembly Line Infrastructure (1929–1933)**
- Conveyer (1929), Commutator segments (1931, 6 countries), Overrunning clutch (1931)

**Precision Manufacturing (1932–1936)**
- Boring machine (1932), IC engine oiling (1933), Crankshaft mold (1934), Welding machine (1934)
- Vehicle wheels (1935), Piston (1935), Piston mold (1935 — with Henry Ford)

**Body/Chassis Engineering (1939–1940)**
- Steering wheels (1939), Vehicle frame (1940)

**Early WWII Mobilization (1941–1942 Q1)**
- Rotor casting (1941), Centrifugal casting (1941 — with Henry Ford)
- Supercharger + Turbosupercharger — same day Feb 9, 1942 (64 days after Pearl Harbor)

**Peak War Production (1942 Q2–Q4)**
- Mold, IC engine (with Henry Ford), Bearing (with Henry Ford), Multiple centrifugal casting
- Airframe assembly + Aircraft manufacture — same day Oct 24, 1942 (5 weeks after first B-24 rollout)

**Total War Engineering (1943)**
- Vehicle track (tanks), Casting hollow articles, Armor plate quenching, Cylinder construction (Van Ranst), Aircraft fuselage construction — all 1943
- After May 1943: no further patent filings before forced departure March 1944
