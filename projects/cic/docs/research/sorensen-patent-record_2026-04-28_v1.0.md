## Analytical Notes

### Henry Ford as Co-Inventor — 4 US Patents (Corrected from v0.1)

v0.1 identified only US2334916A (Bearing, 1942) as having Henry Ford as co-inventor. Lens.org reveals **four US patents** with Henry Ford listed:

| Patent | Title | Filed | Other Co-Inventors |
|--------|-------|-------|-------------------|
| US2086610A | Piston mold | 1935-07-01 | Reinhold, Herman M.; Collins, Cyril A. |
| US2400495A | Centrifugal cast article | 1941-11-21 | McCarroll, Russell H. |
| US2349157A | Internal-combustion engine | 1942-04-20 | — |
| US2334916A | Bearing | 1942-05-04 | — |

Span: 1935–1942. The earliest (Piston mold, 1935) pre-dates Henry Ford's documented cognitive decline — evidence of genuine technical collaboration over a seven-year period. The two 1942 patents (IC engine, Bearing) are from the same period as Willow Run production ramp-up.

### Aviation Patent Cluster — Willow Run Direct Evidence

Three aircraft-specific patents filed 1942–1943, all Willow Run / B-24 Liberator production era:

| Patent | Title | Filed | Co-Inventor | Significance |
|--------|-------|-------|-------------|-------------|
| US2378043A | Method of airframe assembly | 1942-10-24 | Smith, Roscoe M. | Assembly process for B-24 fuselage sections |
| US2383225A | Aircraft manufacture | 1942-10-24 | Smith, Roscoe M. | Same-day filing — coordinated assembly system |
| US2472317A | Method of aircraft fuselage construction | 1943-05-17 | Miller, Logan C. | Structural fuselage fabrication method |

US2378043A (airframe assembly) is cited by 29 subsequent patents — the highest citation count of any Sorensen patent. It was among the most technically influential work of his career.

### The May 17, 1943 Filing Cluster — Corrected (Four Patents, Not Three)

Full-text retrieval of US2428674A confirms a fourth Ford Motor Co patent filed on May 17, 1943 — **not by Sorensen, but by his team**. Three of the four are Sorensen patents; one is by Logan C. Miller and George E. Scarlett:

| Patent | Inventors | Domain | Notes |
|--------|-----------|--------|-------|
| US2378044A | Sorensen, McCarroll, Rothenthaler | Armor | Rouge plant; spray-quench hardening |
| US2378045A | Sorensen, Van Ranst | Aviation engine | B-24 engine cylinders |
| US2472317A | Sorensen, Miller | Airframe — fuselage | Fuselage subcomponent strap method |
| US2428674A | **Miller, Scarlett** | Airframe — wing | Wing internal skeleton jig (NOT a Sorensen patent) |

US2428674A is the direct companion to Sorensen's US2378043A: where Sorensen's Oct 1942 patent covers wing skin assembly, Miller and Scarlett's May 1943 patent covers wing internal skeleton construction. Together they form a complete wing build sequence.

This is the Willow Run engineering team filing their complete B-24 assembly IP in a single coordinated day — Sorensen and four collaborators across three engineering domains: armor, powerplant, airframe.

### Logan C. Miller — Identified

Miller is co-inventor on two patents: US2472317A (fuselage method, with Sorensen) and US2428674A (wing apparatus, with Scarlett). He is the airframe engineering lead on the Willow Run team, bridging wing internal structure and fuselage assembly work. Not yet found in published Willow Run histories. Research priority: Benson Ford Research Center engineering department records.

### George E. Scarlett — New Co-Inventor Identified

Scarlett appears on US2428674A (wing apparatus) with Miller. Role: wing internal skeleton fixture design. Not found in any other documentation. Same research priority as Miller.

### Serial No. 463,268 — "Mating Fixture" (Abandoned Application)

Referenced in both US2428674A ("copending") and US2472317A ("abandoned"). This application described how the strap rings formed in fuselage assembly are used to mate completed fuselage components to adjacent components. The application was not granted. Its abandonment means the mating step of the B-24 fuselage assembly lacks a corresponding issued patent — potentially explaining why this stage is under-documented in Willow Run technical histories. Worth searching USPTO abandoned application records.

### Cornelius Van Ranst — New Co-Inventor

Van Ranst (US2378045A, cylinder construction, 1943) was a significant figure — engineer on the Miller racing cars, collaborator on the Cord front-drive, and later on early automotive engine development. His appearance as Sorensen's co-inventor on a 1943 aircraft engine cylinder patent places him at Willow Run / Ford aviation engine program. Documentary lead.
