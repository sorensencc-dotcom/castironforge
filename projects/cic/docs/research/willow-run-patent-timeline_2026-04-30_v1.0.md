## Patent Filing Timeline vs. Willow Run Phases

### Phase 1: Pre-War / Contract / Site Planning (Mar–Dec 1941)

| Filed | Patent | Relevance |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| 1941-03-31 | US2391523A — Method for casting rotors | Electrical machinery; plant tooling era |
| 1941-11-21 | US2400495A — Centrifugal cast article (w/ Henry Ford, McCarroll) | Foundry process for early war parts |

**Interpretation:** Two patents filed as the plant was being built. Sorensen was simultaneously designing the physical plant AND inventing manufacturing processes for it.

---

### Phase 2: Pearl Harbor to First Aircraft (Dec 1941–Sep 1942)

This is the densest patent cluster. Seven US patents filed in ~8 months while the plant was still coming online.

| Filed | Patent | Relevance |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| 1942-02-09 | US2366365A — Supercharger | B-24 powerplant; Pratt & Whitney R-1830 and R-2800 supercharger systems |
| 1942-02-09 | US2397816A — Exhaust turbosupercharger | Same day; altitude performance for B-24 bomber operations |
| 1942-02-25 | US2383224A — Mold | General foundry tooling |
| 1942-04-20 | US2349157A — Internal-combustion engine (w/ Henry Ford) | Engine systems integration |
| 1942-05-04 | US2334916A — Bearing (w/ Henry Ford) | Drivetrain/engine bearing under high production load |
| 1942-05-23 | US2378042A — Multiple centrifugal casting (w/ McCarroll) | High-volume casting method for war parts |

**Interpretation:** The Feb 9, 1942 dual supercharger/turbosupercharger filing is 64 days after Pearl Harbor. These represent Sorensen's team solving B-24 engine altitude performance problems in real time under AAF pressure. Henry Ford appears on two patents in a 14-day window (Apr 20 and May 4) — his last documented engineering involvement before declining health.

---

### Phase 3: First Aircraft — Ramp-Up Crisis (Sep–Dec 1942)

| Filed | Patent | Relevance |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| 1942-10-24 | US2378043A — Method of airframe assembly (w/ Smith) | **Filed 5 weeks after first B-24 rollout** — process patents for scaling what worked on unit #1 |
| 1942-10-24 | US2383225A — Aircraft manufacture (w/ Smith) | Same day; complementary assembly system documentation |

**Interpretation:** The first B-24 rolled out in September 1942. Five weeks later, Sorensen and Roscoe M. Smith filed two same-day patents on the assembly methods used. This is the production engineer's move: once something works at prototype scale, lock down the IP before scaling. These patents codify Willow Run's assembly line technique for the B-24 at the precise moment the plant was failing to hit rate targets.

---

### Phase 4: Rate Ramp / War Production Pressure (Jan–May 1943)

| Filed | Patent | Relevance |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| 1943-01-28 | US2391524A — Vehicle track construction (w/ Sheldrick) | Tank/armored vehicle production; Rouge plant parallel track |
| 1943-02-12 | US2374902A — Method and mold for casting hollow articles (w/ Reinhold) | Casting tooling improvements during ramp-up |
| 1943-05-17 | US2378044A — Armor plate quenching (w/ McCarroll, Rothenthaler) | Rouge armor production; tank hull hardening |
| 1943-05-17 | US2378045A — Cylinder construction (w/ Van Ranst) | Aircraft engine cylinders — B-24 powerplant engineering |
| 1943-05-17 | US2472317A — Method of aircraft fuselage construction (w/ Miller) | Structural fuselage fabrication |

**Interpretation:** The May 17, 1943 three-patent filing day is the clearest single-day document of the scope of Sorensen's war production operation:
- Armor plate at the Rouge
- Aircraft engine cylinders for Willow Run
- Aircraft fuselage construction at Willow Run

All three domains simultaneously. By May 1943 Willow Run was producing ~1 B-24 per day. These patents represent the process refinements that got it there.

## Summary: What the Filing Pattern Shows

1. **Sorensen was inventing under production pressure, not in advance of it.** The patent clusters track directly onto production crises: the Pearl Harbor acceleration spike (Feb 1942), the first-aircraft-to-scale problem (Oct 1942), and the rate ramp (1943).

2. **The Oct 24, 1942 dual filing is the smoking gun.** Two airframe patents filed five weeks after the first B-24 rolled out. Sorensen was patenting the methods that built the first aircraft while simultaneously scaling them to mass production. That's the assembly line mind applied to aircraft.

3. **Henry Ford's last engineering fingerprint is May 4, 1942** (Bearing patent). After that date, no more Ford co-inventions. His documented decline became severe in late 1942–1943. The patent record independently dates the end of his active technical participation.

4. **The 1943 cluster ends before Sorensen's forced departure (March 1944).** No patents filed between May 1943 and his exit. Possible explanations: (a) administrative battle with Bennett consumed his attention; (b) processes were now too refined/mature to patent; (c) post-1943 innovations were classified war material. Worth investigating.

5. **Roscoe M. Smith** appears on both Oct 24, 1942 aviation patents. He is the key Willow Run engineering collaborator to identify. Not documented in current research files.

6. **Van Ranst's cylinder patent (May 1943)** connects Willow Run to pre-war racing/engine development lineage. Van Ranst built Miller and Cord engines. His 1943 appearance alongside Sorensen suggests Ford recruited elite engine designers for the B-24 powerplant program.
