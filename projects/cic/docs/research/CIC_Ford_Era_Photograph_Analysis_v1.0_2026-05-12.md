### Tags

### Kroll Archive Cross-Reference

**Contextual matches (not direct):**

- **Hicks Reminiscences (#36):** Hicks identifies P.E. Martin as Highland Park superintendent and describes CESOR's 'pencil trick' on the dynamometer — setting a pencil on end to demonstrate engine balance, rushing through a rough patch at 900 RPM. The production floor setting in this image is consistent with the Highland Park dynamometer room era. Hicks also notes: "Henry Ford liked Sorensen because he had a way of getting things done." This photo visualizes the environment Hicks is describing.

- **Bricker Oral History (#39):** Bricker states "from 1921 forward, with manufacturing moving to the Rouge, Mr. Sorensen is in ascendancy." Wheel staging and open-body cars in this photo are consistent with pre-Rouge-era (pre-1921) Highland Park production. Bricker also notes CESOR would "physically grab people" on the floor — this type of crowded, close-quarters assembly environment is the setting.

- **Robinson Reminiscences — M7 Director (#33):** CESOR calls the author directly to "come to Highland Park immediately" — confirming CESOR had direct command authority over Highland Park production floor operations during the war years.

**No direct Kroll entry documents this specific photograph. Recommend cross-referencing with THF Highland Park photograph holdings (Accession 38: CESOR Office Files 1915–1945) to attempt a date and room match.**

### Tags

### Kroll Archive Cross-Reference

**Contextual matches (not direct):**

- **THF140538 — Fifteen Millionth Ford, May 26, 1927 (#4):** The Kroll Log confirms the Fifteen Millionth Ford event took place on "the last day of Model T production." The closed-body Model T cars visible in IMG_1147 are consistent with the 1921–1927 production era — the period leading up to the end of the Model T line. This image may document final-years Model T production, potentially at the same Highland Park facility where the Fifteen Millionth Ford ceremony took place.

- **Hicks Reminiscences (#36):** Hicks's pencil-on-dynamometer anecdote specifically concerns engine smoothness testing — the kind of QC work that preceded final line assembly like what's visible here. "Knudsen famously stood on a running engine, not realizing it was running, it was so smooth."

- **My Forty Years with Ford (#47):** CESOR's memoir documents his role as the man who physically designed and imposed the moving assembly line discipline. This image — a fully operational moving line with vehicles in sequence — is a direct visual representation of what CESOR built.

**No direct Kroll entry documents this specific photograph. Recommend cross-referencing with THF Highland Park final assembly line photograph holdings. The closed-body Model T configuration narrows the date window to 1923–1927.**

Image dimensions and file sizes suggest phone-camera captures of prints rather than flatbed scans — resolution is sufficient for research use but may need higher-quality scans for documentary broadcast.

IMG_1148 (Fifteen Millionth Ford) likely exists in a higher-quality version at The Henry Ford under THF140538. IMG_1149 contains an archival red stamp in the upper-left corner that may provide catalog information — a high-resolution crop of that corner is recommended.
